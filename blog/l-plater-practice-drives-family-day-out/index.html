L-plater practice drives: 6 family days out from Melbourne

9 October 2026 · Family Day Out · teens, learner drivers, weekend

If you have a 16 or 17 year old on their L plates, you already know the maths. In Victoria, learners under 21 need 120 hours of supervised driving, including 20 hours at night, logged in their learner log book or the myLearners app, and they have to hold their permit for at least 12 months before the test (Transport Victoria).

That's a lot of trips to the supermarket. One way to make the hours count, and get the whole family out of the house, is to put your learner behind the wheel for the drive to a day out. The younger kids get a park or a beach, and your teen gets real-world practice on roads they'll actually use.

Here are six drives from Melbourne, from easiest to hardest.

1. Braeside Park: quiet suburban roads (easy)

Start here if your learner is new. The drive to Braeside Park from most south-eastern suburbs is on suburban streets and main roads with traffic lights, roundabouts and plenty of space to practise turning. The park itself has big open grassland, a playground and walking paths, so younger kids can run while your teen has a break.

Practise: roundabouts, left and right turns at lights, parking in a big car park.

2. Point Cook Coastal Park: a first freeway run (easy to medium)

From the inner west or the city, Point Cook Coastal Park is a short trip down the Princes Freeway. It's a good first freeway drive because the entry and exit ramps are long and the traffic is usually lighter on weekend mornings. At the end there's a beach, wetlands and bird hides.

Practise: merging onto a freeway, holding a steady speed, changing lanes, taking an exit.

3. Werribee Park: a longer freeway drive (medium)

Push the freeway practice out a bit further with Werribee Park, with its formal gardens and big lawns. Count it as about an hour of logbook time each way from the eastern suburbs. Pack a picnic and make a day of it.

Practise: longer freeway stretches, keeping a safe gap to the car ahead, reading overhead signs early.

4. The Bay Trail and Albert Park: city driving (medium)

City driving is its own skill: trams, cyclists, pedestrians and lots of parking signs. Drive to Albert Park on a quiet Sunday morning, park, then walk or ride part of the Bay Trail along the foreshore.

Practise: tram lanes and hook turns (in the CBD), watching for bikes, reverse and parallel parking.

5. Dandenong Ranges: hills and bends (harder)

The winding roads up to Olinda are where learners build real confidence: steep hills, tight bends and sometimes fog. Aim for the Dandenong Ranges Botanic Gardens or the R J Hamer Arboretum for a walk among the tall trees, and come down before dark the first few times. If your teen is learning in a manual, this is the place for hill starts.

Practise: hill starts, braking on downhill bends and keeping left on narrow roads.

6. Arthurs Seat: a full day trip (hardest)

Once your learner has the basics, a trip down the Peninsula to Arthurs Seat State Park combines everything: freeway, country roads and a steep, winding climb to the lookout. It's a long day, so share the driving and take breaks.

Practise: long drives, managing tiredness, overtaking safely on country roads, steep hills.

Night hours without the stress

The 20 night hours are often the hardest to fit in. Shorter evening drives on familiar roads work well: the drive home from a late afternoon at the park counts, as long as it's after dark. Start in winter, when it gets dark early.

Tips for the supervising parent

  • Your supervisor licence must be a full car licence, and you sign each trip in the log book or app.
  • Plan the route before you leave so you're not giving directions at the last second.
  • Keep the car calm: younger kids with headphones or a book help a lot.
  • Swap drivers on the way home if your learner is tired.

When to book a lesson

Parents are great for building hours, but a few lessons with a qualified instructor help with the things that are hard to teach from the passenger seat, like test routes, clutch control in a manual car and breaking bad habits early. If your teen wants to drive a manual, book a manual instructor: in Victoria, if they pass the test in an automatic they can't drive a manual until they hold a full licence or pass another test in a manual car (Youth Central). You can find manual driving instructors in Melbourne on our sister site, Manual Driving Lessons.
